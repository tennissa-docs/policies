# Legal Review Notes — Policy Manual v4.0 → v5.0

**Prepared:** October 2026 · **Reviewed:** TENNISSA Policy and Procedures Manual, Version 4.0 (seven policies)

> **Read this first.** This review was prepared by Claude, an AI assistant, working in the role of nonprofit counsel. It is a working draft meant to make your attorney's review faster and less costly. It is **not legal advice** and does not create an attorney-client relationship. Section 25F is new law, and IRS procedures are still being issued. **Before adoption, a licensed Tennessee nonprofit attorney and a CPA should review the manual.** Every date and dollar figure should be confirmed against current IRS and TDOE guidance.

---

## 1. Overall assessment

Version 4.0 is a solid general-purpose nonprofit policy set. It is well organized, uses sensible approval mechanics, and is careful about recusal. Its main weakness is that it was not written around **Section 25F**, the federal law that gives TENNISSA its reason to exist. Several provisions would either break that law or keep the program from working at all.

| Severity | Count | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Critical: legal or program-breaking risk | 6 | Revenue-based ED pay; scholarships to disqualified persons only "recused"; EFS/ESA exclusion; no segregated 25F account; Category III-only plus CISNA may leave fewer than two eligible schools; no 25F donor-statement or reporting duties |
| 🟠 High: compliance gap | 8 | No income test (300% AMGI); no renewal/sibling priority; no earmarking ban; anonymous gifts; non-cash/corporate gifts treated as creditable; no audit; whistleblower path when founders are implicated; TN quorum rule for conflicts |
| 🟡 Moderate: best practice | 10+ | FERPA consent, breach-notice timing, vendor agreements, § 170(f)(8)/§ 6115 acknowledgments, 10-year retention for 25F records, public-disclosure duties, charitable-solicitation registration |

## 2. Critical findings

### C1. Executive Director pay "based on annual scholarship funds administered" (FIN-001 §11, COI-001 §11)
- **Risk:** Paying an insider a share of charitable revenue is a classic private-inurement and excess-benefit concern (IRC §§ 501(c)(3), 4958; Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-4(a)(4) on revenue-sharing). Here the Executive Director is also a permanent Founding Director, which makes the risk worse. Donors who expect their dollars to fund scholarships would also find it hard to accept.
- **Change:** The formula is removed. Pay must be a fixed, reasonable amount, approved in advance by qualified directors using comparability data and contemporaneous documentation (the § 4958 rebuttable presumption). It is paid only from the operating account.

### C2. Disqualified persons (new: SCH-001 §4.6, COI-001 §4)
- **Risk:** Version 4.0 only required conflicted people to *recuse* when an applicant was related to them. Section 25F makes the children and family of directors, officers, selection-committee members and substantial contributors **ineligible**, and recusal does not change that.
- **Change:** This is now an absolute bar, screened every year. **This directly affects the founders' own families.**

### C3. EFS/ESA "qualifies-for" exclusion (SCH-001 §4.2, DON-001 §6, COI-001 §10)
- **Risk:** EFS is open statewide to K–12 students, and income limits only set priority. That means nearly every Tennessee student "qualifies." The rule would shut out most applicants, including the low-income families Section 25F gives priority to. It cannot be administered, because state awards depend on lotteries and caps, and it may conflict with Section 25F's own eligibility and priority scheme.
- **Change:** Replaced with a **coordination (last-dollar) rule**. Families disclose state awards and are encouraged to apply, and the combined awards cannot exceed qualified expenses. A narrower "receiving" alternative is offered as Board decision D3.

### C4. School eligibility: "Category III" only, plus CISNA (SCH-001 §3)
- **Risk 1:** TDOE's Categories I–V describe *how* a school is approved. They are not a ranking. A Category I or II school is not "below" Category III.
- **Risk 2:** CISNA is not among the accrediting bodies TDOE recognizes for Category II/III. Requiring both may leave very few qualifying schools, or none. Section 25F requires scholarships to be available at **more than one school** and to a minimum number of students.
- **Risk 3:** The live website currently says "Category I, II or III" and mentions CISNA candidacy, which does not match v4.0.
- **Change:** Now Category I, II or III **plus** active CISNA accreditation. An optional, time-limited CISNA-candidacy transition is offered as Board decision D1. Added nondiscrimination (race, color, national origin) and a participation agreement.

### C5. Restricted funds tracked "in the accounting records" only (FIN-001 §6)
- **Risk:** Section 25F requires a separate account and a 90% scholarship-spending test over a set period.
- **Change:** A dedicated bank account, a receipt-year FIFO spending schedule, quarterly Board reporting, and dual authorization for every withdrawal from the 25F account.

### C6. No Section 25F operating duties anywhere
- **Change:** Added a new policy, **SGO-001**. It covers state listing and IRS registration, donor statements (Jan 31), the portal contribution report (Feb 28), Form 990 information, the annual independent audit, error correction, rules for public messaging, and oversight of the FACTS Compass platform.

## 3. High-priority findings

| # | Issue | Fix | Where |

| H1 | No income test | Household income must be at or below 300% of area median gross income, verified by IRS-permitted methods or categorical eligibility; no SSN collection | SCH-001 §§4.1–4.2 |
| H2 | No statutory priority | Order is: renewals, then siblings, then need-ranked applicants | SCH-001 §5.1 |

| H6 | No audit requirement | An independent CPA audit every year, overseen by disinterested directors | FIN-001 §10 |
| H7 | Whistleblower route when founders are implicated | A designated independent contact; external channels (IRS Form 13909, Attorney General, Secretary of State, TDOE); timelines | WB-001 §§4, 7 |
| H8 | Tennessee conflict-transaction voting rule | At least 2 qualified directors must approve, with a quorum of at least 2 (T.C.A. § 48-58-703); recommendation to seat independent directors | COI-001 §§7–8 |

## 4. Moderate findings (summary)

- **Records:** Permanent retention for Form 1023, the determination letter, the state SGO listing and Form 990s. **10-year** retention for 25F donor records, because of the 5-year carryforward plus the IRS examination window. Public-disclosure duties. 18 U.S.C. § 1519 caution. A destruction log.
- **Privacy:**
  - FERPA parental consent before schools share records.
  - Tennessee breach statute (T.C.A. § 47-18-2107): notice within 45 days, and notice to consumer reporting agencies when more than 1,000 people are affected.
  - Vendor data-protection agreements.
  - Do not collect SSNs.
  - The website interest form must never collect income documents.
- **Donors:** IRC § 170(f)(8) acknowledgments for gifts of $250 or more; § 6115 disclosure for quid-pro-quo payments over $75. Tennessee charitable-solicitation registration, or the small-charity exemption, available generally when gross contributions are under $50,000 and no paid solicitor is used. Marketing qualifiers for "zero cost / not a donation."
- **Finance:** No loans to insiders; crime/fidelity insurance; reconciliation reviewed by a second person.
- **Whistleblower:** 18 U.S.C. § 1513(e); Tennessee Public Protection Act (T.C.A. § 50-1-304).
- **COI:** Follows the elements of the IRS sample policy, with the § 4958 family definition, an annual periodic review, a gift limit, and an Appendix A form.

## 6. Items for your attorney and CPA to confirm

1. Current IRS deadlines and forms for donor statements, the portal report and Form 990 Section 25F information.
2. The audit threshold and scope under the final regulations.
3. Whether school-designated gifts are permitted (D4).
4. The exact disqualified-person and substantial-contributor definitions and thresholds.
5. Any TDOE rules for SGO certification under Tennessee's 2026 legislation. States may not be more restrictive than Section 25F; confirm what TDOE requires.
6. Whether Bylaws §4.4 (permanent Founding Directors) interacts with COI-001 §8 and WB-001 §4 as intended.
7. Tennessee charitable-solicitation status (registration or exemption) before the first solicitation.
