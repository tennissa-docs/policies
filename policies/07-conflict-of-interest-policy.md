> **Plain-language summary.** Tell the Board about any interest that could affect your judgment, step out of the decision, and let people without a conflict decide. Some conflicts cannot be cured by stepping out. The clearest example is that families of directors and committee members cannot receive scholarships.

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy protects TENNISSA's interests whenever it considers a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of an insider. It is meant to:

- follow the elements of the IRS sample conflict-of-interest policy (Form 1023 instructions);
- support the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness under IRC § 4958 and Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-6;
- meet the director conflict rules of the Tennessee Nonprofit Corporation Act, Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 48-58-701 to 48-58-704; and
- carry out the Section 25F rules on disqualified persons.

This policy supplements, and does not replace, any law that applies.

## 2. Covered Persons

This policy applies to ("Covered Persons"):

- all directors, **including the Founding Directors**, and all officers;
- the Executive Director;
- members of the Scholarship Committee and scholarship reviewers;
- employees, contractors and volunteers with authority over money, scholarships or governance; and
- anyone else exercising delegated authority for TENNISSA.

## 3. Definitions

- **Financial interest.** A direct or indirect ownership or investment interest, a compensation arrangement, or a potential interest in or arrangement with TENNISSA or with any entity or individual TENNISSA does business with, including a participating school.
- **Conflict of interest.** A conflict exists when a Covered Person's financial, family, employment or other interest could improperly influence, or reasonably appear to influence, their judgment on behalf of TENNISSA. A conflict can exist even when no wrongdoing has occurred.
- **Family member.** A spouse, ancestor, sibling (including half-siblings) and the sibling's spouse, children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren, and the spouses of children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren. This follows IRC § 4958(f)(4). It also includes household members and any other person whose interests could reasonably affect the Covered Person's judgment.
- **Related entity.** A business, school or organization in which a Covered Person or family member holds more than a 10% interest or serves as a director, officer, trustee, employee or key decision maker.
- **Disqualified person (Section 25F).** A person defined as a disqualified person under Section 25F and its regulations. See [SCH-001 §4.6](01-scholarship-policy.md).
- **Qualified (disinterested) director.** A director who has no conflict in the matter and is not a family member of, or in a business or employment relationship with, the interested person.

## 4. Conflicts That Cannot Be Cured by Recusal

The following are **prohibited**. Disclosure and recusal do not make them permissible:

1. **A scholarship to a disqualified person.** A Covered Person who is a Section 25F disqualified person may not cause a scholarship to go to themselves or a family member, and no such award may be made at all.
2. **Loans to insiders.** TENNISSA may not lend money to, or guarantee a debt of, any director or officer.
3. **Revenue-based insider pay.** No Covered Person may be paid a percentage of, or by a formula based on, contributions, scholarship volume or revenue.
4. **Self-approval.** No one may approve their own payment, reimbursement, contract or other benefit.

## 5. Duty to Disclose

1. A Covered Person must disclose any actual, potential or apparent conflict, with all material facts, **before** the matter is discussed or decided. Where possible, the disclosure is made in writing to the Board Chair, or to a disinterested director if the Chair is involved.
2. Each Covered Person completes the **annual statement** (Appendix A) when they join and every January. They update it within 30 days of any change.
3. **Standing disclosures** must include:
   - any employment by, board service with, or tuition-paying or ownership relationship with a participating or prospective school;
   - any school-age child, grandchild or other family member who may apply for a TENNISSA scholarship; and
   - any gifts to TENNISSA large enough to make the person a substantial contributor.

## 6. Determining Whether a Conflict Exists

After disclosure, and any discussion with the Board, the interested person leaves the meeting while the qualified directors decide whether a conflict exists. The interested person may answer factual questions before leaving.

## 7. Procedure for Addressing a Conflict

1. The interested person may present factual information. After that, they must leave the meeting during discussion and the vote.
2. If appropriate, the Chair or the qualified directors may appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives.
3. After exercising due diligence, the qualified directors decide whether TENNISSA can obtain, with reasonable effort, a more advantageous transaction that does not involve a conflict.
4. If not, the qualified directors decide whether the transaction is in TENNISSA's best interest, for its own benefit, and fair and reasonable. They then approve or reject it.
5. **Tennessee voting rule.** Approval requires the affirmative vote of a majority of the qualified directors voting, and **at least two** qualified directors must vote in favor (Tenn. Code Ann. § 48-58-703). A quorum for this purpose is a majority of the qualified directors, and never fewer than two. If fewer than two qualified directors are available, the transaction may not proceed unless counsel confirms another lawful route, such as approval by the Attorney General or a court, under § 48-58-702.
6. The interested person may not try to influence the outcome indirectly.

## 8. Board Composition

> *Counsel note.* The Bylaws give TENNISSA's three Founding Directors permanent seats, and one of them is the Executive Director. When a matter involves a founder, only the remaining qualified directors can decide it. If two founders share an interest, such as the founders' own pay or a school all three are connected to, there may be **fewer than two** qualified directors, and the matter cannot be validly approved under § 48-58-703.

1. TENNISSA will make reasonable efforts to fill its two open seats with **independent directors**. These are people who are not employees, not family members of a director, and have no material business relationship with TENNISSA, a director or a participating school.
2. At least one independent director should sit on any committee that sets compensation or audits the books.
3. Until independent directors are seated, conflict matters involving the founders are decided only after obtaining written advice from independent counsel or a CPA.

## 9. Scholarship Applicants and Participating Schools

1. A Covered Person must disclose any personal, family, financial or employment relationship with an applicant. They may not review, score, recommend, approve or administer that application.
2. Covered Persons and their family members who are Section 25F disqualified persons are **ineligible** for scholarships (§4).
3. A Covered Person employed by, serving on the board of, or holding a financial interest in a school must recuse from:
   - approving, reverifying or suspending that school;
   - setting rules that would favor that school; and
   - deciding applications from students at that school.
4. A conflict never waives the school requirements in [SCH-001 §3](01-scholarship-policy.md).

## 10. EFS/ESA/IEA Coordination

A conflicted person may not determine or influence how state-program awards are coordinated under [SCH-001 §4.4](01-scholarship-policy.md) for a student with whom they have a relationship.

## 11. Compensation of Insiders

1. The Executive Director, and any other director or officer who is paid, may not take part in deliberating or voting on their own compensation, benefits, reimbursements or employment terms. They also may not take part in the compensation of a family member.
2. Compensation is approved **in advance** by an authorized body made up entirely of qualified directors (§7.5). To qualify for the § 4958 rebuttable presumption, that body:
   - (a) relies on appropriate **comparability data**, such as pay at similarly sized nonprofits, compensation surveys or written offers;
   - (b) **documents** the decision at the time it is made, recording the terms, the date, the directors present and voting, the comparability data relied on, and any conflicts disclosed. Minutes are prepared before the next meeting, or within 60 days, whichever is later; and
   - (c) sets compensation as a **fixed amount** for a stated period, payable from the **operating account** only.
3. Revenue-based or formula-based compensation is prohibited (§4.3).
4. Directors are not paid for serving as directors.

## 12. Related-Party Transactions

1. TENNISSA avoids related-party transactions when a reasonable alternative exists.
2. A necessary related-party transaction is approved under §7, after considering alternatives and market pricing, and is documented under §15.
3. Related-party transactions are reported on Form 990 as required.

## 13. Donors, Gifts and Confidential Information

1. A Covered Person must disclose any personal benefit or relationship that could affect a decision about accepting a gift or applying a restriction.
2. Covered Persons may not solicit or accept gifts, favors or entertainment worth more than **$100** in a year from any applicant, school, vendor or donor that has business before TENNISSA, other than customary courtesy items.
3. Confidential information may not be used for personal benefit or the benefit of a third party.

## 14. Violations

If the qualified directors have reasonable cause to believe a Covered Person failed to disclose a conflict, they tell the person and give them a chance to explain. If a violation is confirmed, TENNISSA may take corrective action, including:

- reconsidering the affected decision where lawful;
- recovering any excess benefit;
- restricting the person's authority;
- removing them from a committee or office; or
- other action consistent with the Bylaws and the law.

For a Founding Director, the action is limited to what Bylaws Section 4.4 and the law allow. Removal from a **seat** is available only as permitted there, including removal by a court under Tenn. Code Ann. § 48-58-110.

## 15. Records of Proceedings

Minutes of any meeting involving a conflict record:

- the name of the interested person and the nature of the conflict;
- whether the Board found a conflict;
- confirmation that the person left the meeting and did not vote;
- the names of the qualified directors present and how they voted;
- the alternatives and comparability data considered; and
- the decision and the reasons for it.

## 16. Periodic Review

To make sure TENNISSA operates consistently with its charitable purposes and does not engage in activities that could put its tax-exempt status at risk, the Board conducts an **annual review** of:

- whether compensation arrangements are reasonable and based on competent survey information;
- whether arrangements with schools and vendors follow written policies, are properly recorded, reflect reasonable payments and advance TENNISSA's purposes rather than private benefit; and
- whether disqualified-person screening has been carried out.

The Board may use outside advisors for this review. Using them does not relieve the Board of its responsibility.

## 17. Administration

The Board designates a disinterested director, or an independent professional, to collect annual statements, keep the conflicts register, and report to the Board each year.
