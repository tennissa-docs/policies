> **Plain-language summary.** Keep what the law and our tax credit program require for as long as they require it. Destroy everything else securely, on schedule. Never destroy anything once an investigation, audit or lawsuit is possible.

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets standards for creating, keeping, protecting and disposing of TENNISSA's records. It is adopted in part to meet federal law: 18 U.S.C. § 1519, added by the Sarbanes-Oxley Act, applies to nonprofits and makes it a crime to destroy records in order to obstruct a federal investigation. The policy is also part of the governance disclosures TENNISSA makes on IRS Form 990.

## 2. General Requirements

1. TENNISSA keeps records that document its governance, charitable activities, scholarship decisions, finances, donor restrictions and Section 25F compliance.
2. Records may be electronic or paper. They must stay accessible, readable and protected.
3. **Data minimization.** TENNISSA does not collect or keep personal data it does not need (see [Privacy](06-privacy-confidentiality-policy.md) §3).
4. The official copy of records held in TENNISSA's scholarship and donation platform (currently FACTS Compass) is the platform record. Vendor contracts must let TENNISSA export and keep its records for the periods below, including after the contract ends.

## 3. Retention Schedule

Unless a longer period is required by law, contract, grant, donor restriction or a legal hold:

| Record | Minimum retention |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | |
| Articles of Incorporation, Bylaws and all amendments; Board and committee minutes and resolutions; adopted policies (all versions) | Permanent |
| IRS Form 1023 application with attachments, IRS determination letter, and any IRS rulings | Permanent |
| Tennessee SGO approval/listing records, IRS SGO registration confirmations | Permanent |
| Form 990/990-EZ/990-N returns with schedules; Section 25F annual compliance reports and audit reports | Permanent |
| Tennessee annual reports and charitable solicitation registrations or exemptions | 10 years |
| **Section 25F donor records** | |
| Contribution records, Section 25F donor statements/acknowledgments, donor IRS-issued identifiers, IRS SGO portal filings | **10 years** after the end of the year of the contribution (see note) |
| Section 25F account records and 90% spending calculations | 10 years after the last receipt-year group they cover is fully spent |
| **Finance** | |
| General ledger, annual financial statements | Permanent |
| Bank statements, reconciliations, invoices, receipts, payroll, expense reports | 7 years |
| Non-25F donation records and gift restriction documents | 7 years after the fiscal year, or longer while a restriction remains active |
| **Scholarships and schools** | |
| Applications, income verification, residency and disqualified-person screening, EFS/ESA/IEA disclosures, award decisions and payment records | 7 years after the end of the award year. Scholarship records tied to a Section 25F audit year are kept under the 10-year rule. |
| Applications that were denied or withdrawn | 3 years after the decision, unless appealed or subject to a hold |
| School eligibility, accreditation and participation agreements | Throughout participation, plus 7 years |
| **Other** | |
| Contracts and agreements | 7 years after expiration or termination |
| Conflict-of-interest disclosures and related decisions | 7 years after the relationship or transaction ends |
| Compensation approvals and comparability data | 7 years after the compensation ends |
| Personnel files | 7 years after separation |
| Insurance policies | Permanent |
| Whistleblower reports and investigation files | 7 years after closure |
| Privacy/security incidents and breach notices | 7 years after closure |
| Routine correspondence and website interest-form submissions | 3 years, unless related to a legal, financial or program matter |

> *Counsel note on the 10-year rule.* A donor can carry an unused Section 25F credit forward for up to five years. The IRS can then generally examine the return on which the credit was finally used for three years after it is filed. Seven years can therefore be too short to support a donor or TENNISSA in an examination. The Board may shorten the period only on written advice from its CPA.

## 4. Public Disclosure

As federal law requires, TENNISSA makes the following available for public inspection, and provides copies on request:

- its Form 1023 application and determination letter; and
- its three most recent Forms 990, excluding the names and addresses of donors.

Donor identities on Schedule B and Section 25F filings are **not** public. They are protected under the [Privacy Policy](06-privacy-confidentiality-policy.md).

## 5. Legal Holds

1. When litigation, an audit, a government inquiry or an investigation is pending or **reasonably anticipated**, the Records Custodian or counsel issues a written legal hold.
2. Routine destruction of affected records stops at once, including automatic deletion settings in email and platforms.
3. The hold continues until counsel releases it in writing.
4. Anyone who destroys, alters or hides a record covered by a hold, or who does so to obstruct an investigation, is subject to removal from their role and may face criminal liability.

## 6. Secure Destruction

1. Records past their retention period and not under a hold are destroyed during an annual review.
2. Paper records containing confidential information are cross-cut shredded or destroyed through a certified destruction vendor.
3. Electronic records are deleted using methods suited to their sensitivity. Devices are wiped or physically destroyed before disposal.
4. A destruction log records the categories, date ranges, method and who approved each destruction.
5. Destruction must **never** be used to hide misconduct or interfere with an investigation.

## 7. Responsibilities

The Records Custodian runs this policy under Board oversight. Directors who keep TENNISSA records on personal devices or accounts must move them to TENNISSA systems and must return or delete them when their service ends.

## 8. Review

The Board reviews this policy every year.
