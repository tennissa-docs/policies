## 1. Purpose

This policy protects TENNISSA's assets, supports accurate financial reporting, honors donor restrictions, and keeps TENNISSA in compliance with Section 25F, federal tax-exemption rules and Tennessee law.

## 2. General Requirements

TENNISSA will:

- use funds only for authorized charitable purposes;
- keep accurate, complete books under generally accepted accounting principles, or another basis approved by the Board on the advice of its CPA;
- hold Section 25F contributions in a **separate bank account** (§6);
- follow the approved budget and the spending limits in this policy;
- prevent unauthorized transactions, fraud, conflicts and misuse; and
- report regularly to the Board.

## 3. Fiscal Year and Budget

1. The fiscal year is the calendar year, unless the Board decides otherwise. A calendar year matches the Section 25F reporting cycle.
2. The Executive Director and the Financial Controller Director prepare an annual budget. The Board approves it **before** the fiscal year begins.
3. The budget shows the Section 25F account and the operating account separately. It covers projected contributions, scholarship spending needed to meet the 90% requirement (§6.3), operating expenses, professional fees, the audit, insurance and reserves.
4. Material variances (more than 10% or $2,500 in any line, whichever is greater) are reported at the next Board meeting.

## 4. Bank Accounts and Access

1. All accounts are opened in TENNISSA's legal name with its EIN, by Board resolution.
2. Required accounts:
   - **Section 25F Scholarship Account.** This account holds only Section 25F contributions, earnings on them, and school refunds.
   - **Operating Account.** This account holds non-credit gifts, grants and other revenue, and pays administrative costs.
   - Further accounts may be opened only by Board resolution.
3. There must be at least two authorized signers. Every signer must be a director or officer, approved by the Board.
4. **Dual authorization** is required for:
   - every payment or transfer from the Section 25F account;
   - any other payment over $1,000; and
   - any change to bank signers, payees or online banking users.
5. Personal funds and TENNISSA funds may never be mixed. No TENNISSA debit card may be used for personal purchases.
6. Bank statements are reconciled monthly, within 15 days of month-end, by someone who did not initiate or approve the payments. A second person reviews and signs off on the reconciliation.

## 5. Spending Authority

Subject to the approved budget and any Board resolution:

| Transaction | Who may approve |
|---|---|
| Budgeted operating expense up to $2,500 | Executive Director |
| Budgeted operating expense over $2,500 | Executive Director **plus** Financial Controller Director (or another officer) |
| Unbudgeted expense over $500 | Board, or two disinterested directors acting under Board delegation, approved **in writing before** the expense |
| Contracts up to $5,000 and up to 12 months (budgeted) | Executive Director |
| Contracts over $5,000 or over 12 months; any loan, lease, guarantee or line of credit | Board, in advance |
| Scholarship payments | Only for awards approved under the [Scholarship Policy](01-scholarship-policy.md). Payment is released by two authorized people. |
| Any transaction involving an interested person | Disinterested directors only, under the [Conflict of Interest Policy](07-conflict-of-interest-policy.md) |

Splitting a transaction to avoid an approval limit is prohibited. No one may approve their own compensation, reimbursement or any payment in which they have a financial interest.

**No loans to insiders.** TENNISSA will not lend money to, or guarantee a debt of, any director, officer or employee.

## 6. Section 25F Scholarship Account

### 6.1 What goes in

The Section 25F account receives **only**:

- cash contributions from individuals that TENNISSA accepts as Section 25F contributions;
- interest or other earnings on those funds; and
- refunds of scholarship payments from schools.

Corporate, foundation, noncash and pass-through-entity gifts, and any gift TENNISSA does not treat as credit-eligible, go to the operating account.

### 6.2 What goes out

Funds may leave the Section 25F account only for:

1. scholarship payments for eligible students under the [Scholarship Policy](01-scholarship-policy.md); and
2. administrative costs, only to the extent Section 25F and its regulations allow. Under current guidance, at least **90%** of the account's income must go to scholarships within the required time (§6.3), so administrative costs from this account are limited to the remainder.

### 6.3 The 90% spending requirement

1. TENNISSA tracks the account's income by **receipt-year group**. Using first-in, first-out ordering, it confirms that at least 90% of each year's income is spent on scholarships for eligible students **by the end of the second taxable year after the year it was received**, or by whatever deadline Section 25F guidance then requires.
2. The Financial Controller Director prepares a schedule each quarter showing, for each receipt year, the income received, scholarships paid and the remaining amount needed with its deadline. The schedule goes to the Board.
3. If a deadline is at risk, the Board must act at least six months before it, for example by increasing awards or opening a supplemental award cycle.

### 6.4 Investment

Section 25F funds may be held only in federally insured deposit accounts or other low-risk, liquid instruments approved by the Board. Speculative investment is prohibited.

## 7. Receiving Contributions

1. Every contribution is recorded with:
   - date received;
   - amount;
   - method of payment;
   - donor name and address;
   - whether it is treated as a Section 25F contribution; and
   - any restriction.
2. Checks are logged by someone other than the person who records them in the books, where staffing allows.
3. Online payments go through the approved platform (currently FACTS Compass) and are reconciled to bank deposits each month.
4. Donor acknowledgments and Section 25F donor statements are issued as required by the [Donor Acceptance Policy](05-donor-acceptance-policy.md) and the [Section 25F Compliance Policy](08-section-25f-compliance-policy.md). TENNISSA never promises a particular tax result.

## 8. Payments and Reimbursements

1. Every payment needs supporting documents, such as an invoice, receipt, contract or approved award.
2. Reimbursement requests must be submitted within 60 days. They must show the business purpose, date, amount and receipts.
3. No one approves their own reimbursement. The Executive Director's reimbursements are approved by the Financial Controller Director or another disinterested officer.
4. Scholarship payments are released only after the school confirms the student's enrollment for the term.

## 9. Financial Reporting

| Report | Prepared by | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly statement of financial position and activities, by fund, plus bank reconciliations | Financial Controller Director | 15 days after month-end |
| Quarterly report: contributions, awards, Section 25F account balance, 90% spending schedule, budget variances | Executive Director and Financial Controller Director | Next Board meeting |
| Annual financial statements | Financial Controller Director, with outside CPA | Within 120 days after year-end |
| Annual independent audit (§10) | Independent CPA | Per [SGO-001](08-section-25f-compliance-policy.md) calendar |
| Form 990 / 990-EZ with any required Section 25F schedule | Outside CPA, reviewed by Board before filing | Federal due date, including extensions |
| Tennessee charitable solicitation renewal or exemption | Executive Director | Per Secretary of State deadline |

Suspected fraud, material errors, misuse of funds or serious financial concerns are reported **immediately** to the Board Chair, or to a disinterested director if the Chair is involved.

## 10. Audit and Internal Controls

1. **Annual audit.** TENNISSA obtains an annual independent financial audit, including any programmatic or compliance review that Section 25F requires, from a CPA firm with no relationship to any director. The Board, or an Audit Committee of disinterested directors, hires the auditor, receives the report and meets with the auditor without management present.
2. **Separation of duties.** Initiating, approving and recording payments, and reconciling the bank, are done by different people where possible. With a small staff, a disinterested director reviews and documents compensating checks at least monthly.
3. **Insurance.** TENNISSA maintains directors and officers liability insurance and crime/fidelity coverage. The crime coverage is at least equal to the highest expected balance in the Section 25F account, or another amount the Board sets on its broker's advice.

## 11. Compensation and Administrative Costs

> *Counsel note.* Version 4.0 allowed Executive Director compensation "based on annual scholarship funds administered." That is a revenue-based (percentage) arrangement. Paying an insider a share of charitable revenue is a well-known **private inurement and excess-benefit risk** under IRC §§ 501(c)(3) and 4958. It is especially risky here because the Executive Director is also a permanent Founding Director. It also invites the appearance that donor dollars meant for scholarships are paying the founders. We have removed it.

1. Compensation of the Executive Director, any officer, or anyone related to a director must be:
   - **reasonable**, meaning comparable to what similar organizations pay for similar services;
   - approved **in advance** by directors who have no conflict, under [COI-001 §11](07-conflict-of-interest-policy.md);
   - supported by **comparability data** and documented **at the time** of approval, so that it qualifies for the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness under Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-6; and
   - a **fixed** salary or fee set in advance. It may not be a percentage or formula based on contributions, scholarships or revenue.
2. Compensation is paid **only from the operating account**, never from the Section 25F account.
3. No compensation is paid automatically, retroactively or before approval.
4. Directors serve without pay for their service as directors. They may receive reimbursement of reasonable documented expenses.

## 12. Records

Financial records are kept under the [Records Retention Policy](03-records-retention-policy.md).

## 13. Review

The Board reviews this policy every year, after each audit, and whenever Section 25F guidance changes.
